Greenville Natives, 2026 season. Week 10 recap and Week 11 preview.

Now renting

Three weeks left, boys, and apparently Rex and I needed a reminder that nobody gives a shit about your record. Galt beat me, and T Wood absolutely dominated Rex in the Taylor Bowl, 169–57.6, taking the Taylor 1 title by a humiliating 111.4 points. Now I have to publish a set of power rankings with Wood at number one. Tough week for the commissioner. Meanwhile, Waivers has won four straight, Hud is back to .500, and Potter is discovering that having a better team on paper doesn’t stop Ian from lighting that paper on fire. Nine teams are still mathematically alive for the playoffs. Clod is doing some heavy lifting on the word “mathematically.”

Twelve years ago, I compared this same group to supervillains. You would think somewhere between then and now, marriage, children and approaching forty would have given us some character development. Instead, I’m still writing insults about the same nine assholes while convincing myself that maintaining a spreadsheet counts as a public service. This time we’re doing animated movie villains. A group of jealous, manipulative, emotionally unstable men who cannot handle somebody else getting what they want. I barely had to research the characters. I’ve been in a group chat with them for years.

We’ve got a commissioner trying to seize ultimate power, a man who believes an entire village should sing about how great he is, and several managers whose plans could be improved by putting a small animal in charge. Below that, the playoff race is getting tight enough that some of you are about to become very invested in teams you’ve spent ten weeks calling dog shit. Shain is officially out of championship contention, but escaping the Caddy Bowl is still technically possible. Even his happy ending now requires other people to suffer. Finally, a theme that understands this league.

Everyone knew this was going to be Hud. Since his nickname is Captain Hook from his supposed curved pecker. The resemblance ends somewhere around the luxurious head of hair, but we can give him the hat. After beating Clod 150.9–95.3, Hud has won two straight and steered his ship back to .500. For a captain who has spent seventeen seasons searching for championship treasure, finding his way back into the playoff race qualifies as a productive voyage.

DPDak Prescott supplied 34.3, JCJa’Marr Chase scored 28.6, ABAmon-Ra St. Brown added 23.8, and ILIsaiah Likely delivered 23.2. Those four alone outscored Clod’s entire lineup, making the remaining five starters mostly responsible for keeping the rum cold. This was Hud’s second-highest score of the season, and the kind of performance that makes the teams above him wish somebody had sunk this asshole when they had the chance.

But what about that gator swimming behind him with a clock in its mouth? Has Hud run out of time to make a playoff push? Not yet—but he can hear the ticking. Waivers, Trey and Galt remain, starting with a fellow 5–5 manager riding four straight wins. Hud also trails Potter and Waivers in total points, so finishing tied could leave him watching somebody else sail away with the fourth spot. Beat Waivers and he grabs the bastard’s ankle before he climbs aboard. Lose, and suddenly there are only two games left to make up ground. That gator hasn’t swallowed Hud’s season, but it’s close enough that Smee should probably stop polishing the fucking cannon and hand him a paddle.
