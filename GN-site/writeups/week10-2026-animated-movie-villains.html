Greenville Natives, 2026 season. Week 10 recap and Week 11 preview.

Now renting

Three weeks left, boys, and apparently Rex and I needed a reminder that nobody gives a shit about your record. Galt beat me, and T Wood absolutely dominated Rex in the Taylor Bowl, 169–57.6, retaining Taylor No. 1 with his second straight victory by a humiliating 111.4 points. That was Wood’s third-highest regular-season score ever and Rex’s lowest. Now I have to publish power rankings with Wood at number one. Tough week for the commissioner. Meanwhile, Waivers has won four straight, Hud is back to .500, and Potter has faced an opponent’s season-high score four times in ten games. We’re introducing Hermania Bot, the league’s database and statistics assistant, to explain exactly how often the schedule has bent him over.

Congratulations to Galt and Hud on reaching 100 all-time wins. Galt decided his milestone needed to come at my expense, while Hud used Clod’s lineup as the ceremonial ribbon. Shain, Ian and Clod are tied at 97 with three games remaining, so any could become the next member to reach 100 by winning out. For Shain, that means winning three games after spending ten weeks winning one. Nine teams remain mathematically alive for the playoffs, with Clod doing some heavy lifting on “mathematically.” Shain’s remaining happy ending involves escaping the Caddy Bowl, and even that requires other people to suffer.

Twelve years ago, I compared this same group to supervillains. You would think marriage, children and approaching forty would have produced some character development. Instead, I’m still insulting the same nine assholes while convincing myself that maintaining a spreadsheet counts as a public service. This time we’re doing animated movie villains: jealous, manipulative, emotionally unstable men who cannot handle somebody else getting what they want. I barely had to research the characters. I’ve been in a group chat with them for years. Now some of you need help from teams you’ve spent ten weeks calling dog shit. Finally, a theme that understands this league.

Along with retaining the title of Taylor No. 1 by winning his second Taylor Bowl in a row, Wood has also somehow taken over first in the power rankings. This made me go back to the drawing board and look over the power-score formula. The one I created. Which, of course, takes six times the points-for/points-against ratio, twice the sum of the highest and lowest weekly scores, and (wins × 400) / number of weeks, then divides the total by ten. I know you were all curious.

Farquaad needed a tournament to find someone capable of doing his dirty work. Wood held one matchup and discovered Rex couldn’t even survive the opening round.
