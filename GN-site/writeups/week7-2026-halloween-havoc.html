How the odds were calculated
  • Lock in Weeks 1–7. Retain each team’s actual wins, losses, ties and points scored. Calculate its arithmetic scoring mean and sample standard deviation (the sum of squared deviations divided by 6, then square-rooted). No prior seasons, power scores, optimal lineups or projected scores enter those two estimates.
  • Apply a small bye adjustment. There were no 2026 Week 8–13 player projections in the workbook’s projection table. Instead, use current roster NFL affiliations and the confirmed NFL bye schedule. Give each rostered player a proxy equal to the mean of their positive Weeks 1–7 projections; these are historical availability weights, not forecasts for future games. Compare the strongest available legal lineup each week with the average availability of that same current roster under the Weeks 1–7 bye calendar. This reference helps avoid charging again for the typical bye burden already reflected in actual scores.
  • Keep history in control. The weekly scoring mean is: historical mean × [1 + clamp(0.25 × (weekly lineup proxy ÷ reference lineup proxy − 1), −0.10, +0.10)]. Only one quarter of the relative availability change carries through. Actual adjustments range from −4.61% to +0.53%; the historical sample standard deviation stays unchanged. The 25% weight and 10% cap are conservative modeling choices, not fitted or validated coefficients.
  • Allow replacements. The availability calculation fills one QB, two RBs, two WRs, one RB/WR flex, one TE, one D/ST and one kicker. Bench players can fill eligible slots. Generic free-agent options are valued at 60% of the league median positive historical starter projection for that position: QB 11.25, RB 8.46, WR 7.32, TE 5.40, D/ST 4.08 and K 5.46. Players without a matched positive projection receive that fallback; free agents without an NFL team are excluded. These proxy points affect only the small bye adjustment.
  • Simulate complete seasons. Run 10,000 seasons, each containing all 30 remaining matchups. In every week, draw one independent normal score per team using its adjusted mean and historical sample SD; floor negative draws at zero and round scores to one decimal. Each team’s score determines both its matchup result and added points for. Seed: 20261027, NumPy 2.3.5 PCG64. This produces 600,000 team-week scores.
  • Select four qualifiers. Add simulated Weeks 8–13 to actual Weeks 1–7. Rank by record (wins plus half a win per tie), then full-season points scored, and take the top four. A seeded random draw resolves an exact tie in both record and points; this affected the fourth/fifth boundary once in 10,000 seasons. Each team’s odds are its qualifying count divided by 10,000.
  • Check the result. All 70 historical scores agree with the ESPN import; all ten records and points totals agree with the season summary. The schedule agrees across both sources for all 65 regular-season games. Every simulation has four qualifiers, the unrounded odds sum to 400%, rerunning the seed reproduces the results, and an independent ranking implementation agrees for every season.

Assumptions and limits

Seven games are a small sample. The model treats team strength and scoring variability as stable, with independent scores across teams and weeks. It does not forecast injuries, trades, waiver moves, changing roles, weather, NFL opponent effects or lineup mistakes. Current rosters and NFL team labels are taken from the workbook; using them under the earlier bye calendar is an availability reference, not a reconstruction of historical rosters. Historical projections with zero or missing values are excluded from player weights, so this is not an injury adjustment.

Doubling the bye weight to 50% moved Wood to 87.92%, Potter to 82.71%, Waivers to 12.67% and Hud to 13.36%. Those are sensitivity results, not confidence limits. Simulation noise is generally smaller than the uncertainty in the model itself; at 10,000 runs, the worst-case approximate 95% sampling margin is ±0.98 percentage points. The source checks verify the inputs and arithmetic, not the future accuracy of these odds.
