A NOTE FROM THE DRIVE-THRU

Every fast food mascot has a job. The Colonel guards a secret, the Hamburglar steals, and Grimace exists. This week every team in the league got one, and I’m the one who got to pick.

Here’s the headline: for the first time in league history, two teams are 4-0 to start the year. Wood and I both got there the hard way. Sam LaPorta caught a touchdown late on Sunday Night Football to seal mine over Hud, and Juwan Johnson had a couple of grabs in the second half of Monday Night Football to get Wood past Ian by 2.4. Meanwhile Rex put up 160.7, the team with the fourth-best score of the week (Shain) is 0-4, and Waivers left a ruled-out Terry McLaurin in his lineup. Order up.

First time ever. Two teams, Wood and Trey, are 4-0 after four weeks. Nobody in league history had done that before this season.

WOOD / COLONEL SANDERS
Eleven herbs and spices. Nobody sees the recipe.
T Wood × Colonel Sanders
1

Wood 4-0

Colonel Sanders. Power score 90.2, no change.

The Colonel has one job, which is to keep a secret recipe nobody is allowed to see. Wood’s recipe is 4-0, the best luck score in the league at +8.9, and a 97.4 this week that was only the seventh-best score on the board. It came down to Monday Night Football, where Juwan Johnson had a couple of grabs in the second half and that was the edge over Ian, 2.4 points. Wood left 21.2 on his bench (Romeo Doubs had 20.8 of it) and still won, and not one of his players made the all-league nine. That’s the secret: there is no secret, the grease is just working. He and I are the first two teams in league history to start 4-0 in the same season, so for once we can’t blame each other’s luck.

In North Carolina a Bojangles is a sacrament, and Big Bo is the guy who shows up in the drive-thru at 6 a.m. whether you asked for him or not. Sam LaPorta caught a touchdown late to seal it, 93.7 to 83.3. Hud left 18.7 on his bench, so I’d like to thank the football gods and Jaylen Warren’s 14.1 on the pine.

REX / THE BURGER KING
Have it your way.
flame-broiled, crown included
Taylor Hardee × The Burger King
3

Rex 3-1

The Burger King. Power score 83.8, no change.

Have it your way, and Rex has. His 160.7 was a 51.3-point flame-broiling of Shain, and it came with Kenneth Walker III at 30.4, Bijan Robinson at 27.2, Zay Flowers at 21.8, and a kicker at 18.0. His 581.5 points through four weeks are the second-most in league history, one point behind Waivers’ 582.5 in 2011 (table below). His 30-6 power record is the best start since Waivers opened 33-3 in 2022, which means he wins about five out of every six matchups you could draw for him. He has also been the top scorer in three of the four weeks, which has only happened twice before in league history (Waivers in 2011 and Potter in 2014, table below). He’s 3-1 and the best team in the league by every measure that isn’t the standings, and he’s wearing the crown.

Ronald McDonald. Power score 69.0, no change.

Ronald McDonald is the face of the franchise, and nobody has more championships in this league than Mark. He’s 2-2 and has lost two straight, and this week it was 121.2, the third-best score of the week, losing to Lee’s 139.7. Some things never change, and one of them is Mark Potter rostering Alvin Kamara. At this point Kamara isn’t a roster spot, he’s tenure, and the loyalty is positively saintly. Kamara put up 20.3 on the bench while Bucky Irving gave him 6.1 in the lineup. Start Kamara and his running backs are Derrick Henry, Aaron Jones, and Alvin Kamara, all over 30, which would have made Potter the first team in league history to start three running backs over 30. He was one click away from history and chose Bucky Irving. Even his best possible lineup, 137.5, lost to Lee by 2.2. Burrow was the top quarterback of the week and the Seahawks were the top defense, and it still wasn’t enough.

The Arby’s Oven Mitt. Power score 35.8, down 1.

Even his perfect lineup with the trade, 120.1, loses by 40.6. The trade cost him 29.3 points and gave Rex 0.6, which is a hell of a way to lose a game you were going to lose anyway.
