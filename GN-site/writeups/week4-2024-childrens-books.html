2024 · Week 4

Childrens Books

Four weeks into the fantasy football season, and we’re getting a reminder that luck may just play a bigger role than strategy. You can feel the midseason lull creeping in by the lack of group texts. Some of us, like Ian, are coasting along with little effort, while others are feeling the pinch. Just ask Ian, who, despite being the second-highest scorer in Week 3, lost to the highest scorer and didn’t shed a single tear. That’s leadership for you. Not everyone can relate (cough Potter). Meanwhile, Hud and Wood’s teams are quieter than a nap time story hour, and Rex is burning through FAAB faster than you can say "timeout."

Let’s get into it, folks. It’s time to compare our fantasy teams to children’s books because if nothing else, we all deserve a bedtime story after these matchups.

  1. Ian Baro – The Little Engine That Could

How does Ian keep winning? Is it magic? Maybe. Is it skill? Definitely. Ian’s team is a well-oiled machine. Despite some challenges, he’s confidently chugging along like the little engine that never stops. With Justin Jefferson and Jonathan Taylor as keepers, you’d think he’d have this in the bag, but it feels like Ian’s got an extra bit of that “I think I can” energy that makes the rest of us feel a little… engine-less.

  1. Lee Galt – Chicken Little

Forget the sky falling—Lee is soaring. Despite what those dumb ass “analysts” over at TreyCenter said, his Zack Moss for Drake London trade looks genius two weeks later. The doubters have been proven wrong, and Lee’s team is laying golden eggs. His team's success isn’t based on fear or panic, but on confidence and good timing. While others were looking up, afraid the sky was falling, Lee just kept moving forward.

  1. Trey Wilder – Where the Wild Things Are

Trey's team feels like it's constantly on the edge of chaos, but somehow, they thrive there. Every week is a wild ride, and the beasts on his roster (Saquon Barkley, anyone?) know how to throw a party on the scoreboard.

  1. Shain Collins – Jack and the Beanstalk

Shain's season started out tough, but like Jack, he's been climbing ever since. Despite his and Miami PD’s struggles with the Cheetah (Tyreek Hill), draft day steals Brian Robinson Jr., Nico Collins, and Jayden Reed have been his magic beans, and he's soared into the clouds. With matchups against Rex sandwiched with a match against Ian looming, this team looks like it can steal a win or two from the giants of the league. Shain’s roster might have looked scrappy at first, but he’s making those magic beans work for him—and the rest of us should be worried about what happens when he reaches the top. Watch out, giants!

  1. Taylor Hardee – The Very Hungry Caterpillar

Rex is chomping through FAAB like it’s a buffet, and we all remember his freshman-year Pirate Bucks spree at the dining hall. Despite spending $46 already, his tight end room (currently three of them!) ranks 8th in the league. If Rashee Rice has avoided a major injury, Rex’s team might just be getting through its "hungry" phase, waiting to transform into a beautiful butterfly—or at least a team that isn’t eating up all its resources before Week 5.

  1. Michael Waivers – Hansel and Gretel

Let me be the first to congratulate Waivers on being the second league member to reach 100 regular season wins! With 34 points, Waivers will also become the 4th member of the 20k points club. Waivers didn’t fall into any traps this season. He knew better than to trust strangers—or injury-prone players like CMC. With CeeDee Lamb and Christian McCaffrey as his anchors, Waivers cleverly drafted Jordan Montgomery, who has been his breadcrumb to success. Now, he’s not just surviving; he’s thriving. Waivers is steering his team away from mediocrity and toward a solid path to victory.

  1. Mark Potter – Alexander and the Terrible, Horrible, No Good, Very Bad Day -

Sometimes the low-hanging fruit is just too easy. Potter’s season has had more “terrible, horrible” moments than we can count. Sometimes life just hands you a rough day, or in Potter’s case, a rough season/life. Like Alexander, everything seems to be going wrong for Mark. His team can’t catch a break, and week after week, it feels like his fantasy fortunes are stuck in the “terrible, horrible, no good” zone. It’s almost too easy to make this comparison—but hey, every story has its down moments. Let’s hope Potter’s team finds its way out of this bad day sooner rather than later. Sorry, Mark, but at least this book has a happy ending… eventually.

  1. Cody Clodfelter – If You Give a Mouse a Cookie

Every move Clod makes seems to have a ripple effect. Losing Puka Nacua was a tough pill to swallow, but the pickup of Rasheed Shaheed has kept his team afloat. Like the mouse in the book, every decision Clod makes opens up new challenges—but as long as he keeps cleaning up his messes, he might just make it through this season in one piece. Clod faces off with Ian this week, which Ian has the upper hand 11-3 all-time. With a loss Clod will tie Hud (Potter) for worst winning percentage against another team.

  1. T Wood – The Ugly Duckling

Poor Wood. This isn’t the first or last time he’ll be referred to as the ugly duckling. On paper, Wood’s team should be better than it is. But like the Ugly Duckling, his roster just hasn’t blossomed into what it could be. Is it the kicker? Justin Tucker, currently the 25th highest-scoring kicker after four weeks, isn’t exactly helping his case. But there’s hope that Wood’s team is just a late bloomer—ready to shake off its ugly start and emerge as something much better. His showdown with Hud this week could be a turning point for both teams, and the question remains: will this duckling become a swan?

  1. Josh Hudson – The Giving Tree

Hud has been giving away wins this season like the Giving Tree handed out its branches. Dating back to last season, Hud has now lost eight straight. His generosity is becoming a problem, as he’s racking up losses and could fall even further if he doesn’t turn things around. A loss this week would tie his personal worst for losses in a row at 9 and put him one away from Shain’s league record(10) for straight losses. This weeks matchup against Wood could be a crucial turning point in the Caddy standings. A loss here, combined with a Potter win, would push Hud two games back... or is it ahead? Either way, Hud needs something—preferably wins—soon.

So, here we are. Four weeks in, and some teams are looking strong, while others are paging through the playbook hoping for a plot twist. Keep your heads up, because like any good children's book, there’s always a lesson learned by the end!
